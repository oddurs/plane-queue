Where the aisle jammed

Passenger-seconds spent blocked, by row across and time downward, aligned with the cabin above. A jam forms behind whoever is stowing, so congestion shows up forward of its cause: back-to-front lights the front of the cabin, because that bright block is the queue backed up toward the door.

Strategy ranking

Runs every strategy over the same passengers and seeds. Bars show the median, whiskers the interquartile range. Where whiskers overlap, the difference is not resolved at this many trials.

When does it matter

Sweeps one parameter and re-runs every strategy at each value. The crossings and convergences are the point: a ranking that only holds at one operating point is not a recommendation.

Search for a better policy

Steffen found his optimum by Markov Chain Monte Carlo on an idealised model. This runs the same kind of annealing search against the full simulator, so the answer respects the constraints you have set. The winner is re-scored on seeds the search never saw, and a margin is only called a win if it clears the noise.

Read the result as a statement about this model, not about aircraft. Where it beats Steffen it does so partly by packing the aisle more densely than his two-row spacing allows — this simulator charges a flat penalty for stowing beside someone rather than modelling how much two people at neighbouring bins truly obstruct each other.

The debrief

Changes one thing at a time against your current settings and measures what it is worth — which lever actually moves boarding time here, and which only looks like it does. Effects inside the run-to-run noise are reported as such rather than dressed up.

The bench

Runs kept aside so they can be argued about. Pinning samples the scenario properly rather than saving a single boarding, so any two pins can be compared under the same noise test as every other claim here.

Calibration check

Runs the five methods on the mock 757 the engine is fitted to and compares against the published times.
